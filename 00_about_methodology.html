GDELT Methodology & Codebook

Baltic hybrid-threat reporting and institutional-response visualisation suite

Project overview

This dashboard visualises a classified GDELT dataset covering reporting and event records associated with Lithuania, Latvia and Estonia between 24 February 2022 and 25 September 2026.

The dataset is designed as a broad, high-recall observation layer rather than a definitive database of confirmed hybrid-threat incidents. GDELT records are therefore treated as indicators of reporting/event activity which can subsequently be investigated and triangulated against other sources.

Incident Database is the event-level layer used for incidence analysis. It contains Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania and Regional sheets. Routine patrols are excluded from the database; Regional non-air-policing incidents are assigned to the most relevant Baltic country for country-comparable analysis.

Conceptual framework

The threat classification draws primarily on the European Commission Joint Research Centre and Hybrid CoE conceptualisation of hybrid threats.

The framework identifies multiple domains and forms of activity through which actors may pursue political, security, economic or other objectives.

The classifier operationalises selected concepts from this framework for use with GDELT event data and adds several project-specific HT categories for recurring Baltic security phenomena.

Important interpretation rule:

A category label does not by itself establish that a record represents a confirmed hybrid threat. Military exercises, espionage reporting, migration, infrastructure incidents, diplomatic measures and other activities may occur independently of hybrid warfare.

The classification therefore identifies records associated with a particular type of activity. Whether an activity constitutes a hybrid threat requires contextual interpretation, including consideration of actor, intent, coordination, attribution and the relationship between different activities.

JRC and HT categories

JRC categories are operationalisations of concepts drawn from the JRC/Hybrid CoE hybrid-threat framework.

HT categories are additional project-specific categories introduced to capture recurring threat phenomena that were especially relevant to the Baltic/GDELT dataset, including drones, balloons, sabotage, espionage, FIMI and border-related activity.

Institutional-response codebook

The response layer is a separate derived classification. A GDELT record can therefore simultaneously contain a threat classification and a response classification. The response layer is intended to identify GDELT-observed institutional-response reporting signals, not unique real-world institutional responses.

Baltic relevance

The classifier uses three Baltic relevance tiers:

Tier Meaning
A - DIRECT The record has a direct geographic connection to Lithuania, Latvia or Estonia.
B - ACTOR/CONTEXTUAL A Baltic actor, institution, state or security context is involved, but the underlying event is not necessarily geographically located in the Baltic states.
C - BACKGROUND The record contains Baltic-related context but does not provide a sufficiently direct connection for the principal incident.

Confidence

Threat and response classifications are assigned confidence levels according to the classifier's detection logic. Confidence describes the strength of the classification signal; it does not mean that the underlying event itself has been independently verified.

How the visualisations are calculated

01 — Overall hybrid-threat incidence

Monthly count of unique incidents in Incident Database. All four database sheets are included, including Regional airspace/scramble/ intercept observations. Multi-classified incidents are counted once here.

02 — Threat families

Incident Database incidents are expanded into their assigned analytical families. A multi-family incident appears in every relevant family; this therefore measures family incidence rather than unique incidents across families.

03 — Lithuania / Latvia / Estonia / Regional

Country series use incidents assigned to Lithuania, Latvia or Estonia. Regional incidents that are clearly NATO air-policing scrambles/intercepts remain in a separate Regional series. Other Regional incidents are assigned to the most directly identifiable Baltic country; residual Regional cases are assigned to Lithuania as a conservative Baltic-flank fallback.

04 — Incident category × month heatmap

Each incident is counted once in every JRC/HT category attached to it. The heatmap therefore preserves multi-classification rather than forcing each incident into one mutually exclusive category.

05 — Incident category small multiples

The selected category panels show actual Incident Database incidence. A multi-classified incident appears in each relevant category panel.

06 — Database view

An HTML viewer exposes the four analytical Incident Database sheets: Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania and Regional. The Change Log, Source Comparison and Methodology & QA sheets are intentionally excluded from the viewer.

07 — Baltic context analysis

This retains the GDELT Baltic relevance analysis, distinguishing records with direct Baltic geographic relevance from records where a Baltic actor or context is present without direct event geography.

08 — Reporting category × month heatmap

This is the GDELT reporting layer rather than the incident layer. The September 2025 JRC-AIRSPACE observation is displayed separately so that its unusually large reporting volume does not dominate the colour scale for other cells.

09 — Reporting categories overlaid with actual incidents

GDELT reporting series are shown together with actual Incident Database incidence for the same categories. The two measures should not be treated as equivalent: repeated reporting can produce many GDELT observations for one real-world incident.

10 — Actual threat incidence versus relevant response

Actual incident incidence is compared with the number of incidents that have at least one temporally and categorically matched GDELT threat-reporting observation and with the number that have a matched GDELT institutional- response observation. Matching uses a ±3-day window and overlapping threat category or analytical family. This is correspondence, not proof that an individual article describes the exact incident.

11 — Incident category × matched response category

The heatmap uses actual incident classifications as the threat side and only GDELT response records matched to those incidents under the same ±3-day/category-family rule.

12 — Incident incidence versus response intensity

Response intensity is recalculated at the actual-incident level. The analytical weights are applied to matched GDELT response categories and then averaged across actual incidents, including incidents with no matched response. Country totals exclude Regional incidents that are solely NATO air-policing scrambles/intercepts.

13 — Response intensity over time

Shows mean event-level weighted response intensity over time by incident threat family. Multi-family incidents contribute to every family they carry.

14 — Threat reporting robustness

Compares classified GDELT threat-record counts with unique SOURCEURL counts, indicating how much the reporting signal may reflect repeated coverage rather than independent source URLs.

15 — Response reporting robustness

Applies the same classified-record versus unique-source comparison to explicitly threat-linked GDELT response reporting.

16 — Incident incidence versus GDELT reporting

Compares actual hybrid-threat incidence with overall GDELT hybrid-threat reporting. The two series are shown directly without an imposed average or linear trend.

Response-intensity weighting

The response visualisations use an analytical weighting scheme to distinguish different types of institutional response. Higher weights are assigned to military, investigative, attribution, cyber and sanctions responses; lower weights are assigned to warnings and general speeches. For the incident-based analysis, these weights are applied only after a GDELT response record has been matched to an Incident Database event within ±3 days and by threat category or family.

The resulting index should therefore be interpreted as a relative analytical measure constructed from the classifier, not as a measurement of the actual magnitude or effectiveness of an institutional response.

Important limitations

  • GDELT records are reporting/event observations rather than a verified incident database.
  • Multiple GDELT records may describe the same underlying real-world event.
  • A high number of records can therefore reflect either a large number of events or extensive reporting about a smaller number of events.
  • Some categories necessarily contain contextual or borderline cases.
  • Category counts should not be interpreted as counts of confirmed Russian hybrid attacks.
  • Response records represent observed reporting about institutional responses, not a complete inventory of actions taken by governments or organisations.
  • Temporal correlation does not establish causation.

Recommended interpretation

The principal analytical use of this dataset is to identify temporal patterns, concentrations and unusual reporting spikes.

When a category or period shows an unusual increase, the appropriate next step is to inspect the underlying GDELT records and reconstruct the relevant real-world incident(s) or episode(s), followed by triangulation against independent sources.

The dashboard should therefore be understood as an event/reporting signal and discovery tool, rather than as a definitive measurement of the incidence of hybrid warfare.

Reproducibility

The visualisations are generated directly from the classified GDELT dataset and the derived institutional-response layer. The dashboard does not manually alter individual observations for presentation purposes, with the documented exception of the September 2025 JRC-AIRSPACE cell in the heatmap colour scale.
